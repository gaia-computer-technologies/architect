# Planning sources by jurisdiction

_Last reviewed: 2026-11. Sources move and change coverage. If this is much older than today, say so and re-check each link before relying on it._

Use these as starting points for retrieval. Always record the dataset or page date you used.

## United Kingdom

- **England — planning.data.gov.uk** (https://planning.data.gov.uk/docs). Official, keyless API with an OpenAPI specification. Aggregates datasets such as conservation areas, listed buildings, Article 4 directions, tree preservation zones, and more. Coverage depends on what each local planning authority has published, so check the dataset's entry date and the authority's own records for anything decisive.
- **Local planning authority planning portals.** Each authority runs its own application search (often a public-access system). Use it for planning history, decision notices, officer reports, and appeal references.
- **Local plans and supplementary guidance.** Published on the authority's website. Note adoption date and whether an emerging plan is in examination.
- **Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland** have separate planning systems and portals. Don't apply English policy there.

## United States

- **Municipal zoning codes.** Usually published online by the municipality or through a code-hosting service. Quote the section and the code's effective date.
- **Municipal open-data portals** (many run on Socrata/SODA, e.g. NYC Open Data) for parcels, zoning districts, landmarks, and permits. Prefer the API or dataset page over third-party wrappers.
- **Zoning maps and overlays** from the city or county GIS department.
- **Historic districts and landmarks** from the local preservation commission; the National Register of Historic Places for federal listings.

## European Union and elsewhere

- **National and regional cadastres** for parcel boundaries and ownership references.
- **Municipal land-use and zoning plans** (names vary: Bebauungsplan / Flächennutzungsplan in Germany, bestemmingsplan / omgevingsplan in the Netherlands, PLU in France, PGOU in Spain, and so on). Retrieve the plan in force for the parcel and record its adoption date.
- **National heritage registers** for listed or protected monuments.
- **Flood and environmental maps** from the national environment agency.

## Mapping helpers

- **Google Maps Platform "Grounding Lite" MCP** (official; place search, routes, weather) and **Mapbox MCP** (official) can help locate a site. They are not planning sources.

## What not to use as a source

- Third-party summaries, estate-agent listings, or forum posts, except as a pointer to an official record.
- Your own memory of a designation or zone. Retrieve it, or list it as "To retrieve".
